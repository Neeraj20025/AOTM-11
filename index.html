01. The Conceptual Spark: History of AI

From philosophical theories to the first functional programs, discover the intellectual lineage of AI.

1950: The Turing Test

Alan Turing proposes the fundamental question: can machines think? Establishing the benchmark for machine intelligence.

1956: The Dartmouth Workshop

The field is officially named "Artificial Intelligence." Key founders (McCarthy, Minsky, Shannon) gather to formalize the research area.

1970s: The First AI Winter

Over-optimistic predictions combined with limited computing power led to significant cuts in government funding for fundamental AI research.

1980s: Expert Systems Resurgence

Focused, rule-based systems saw commercial success in specific domains (e.g., medical diagnosis), leading to a brief, second wave of investment.

Key Takeaway: The Symbolic Era

Early AI centered on **symbolic reasoning** and logical rules (GOFAI - Good Old-Fashioned AI), attempting to explicitly program human thought processes.

02. The Data Tide: Evolution into Deep Learning

The journey from rigid rules to adaptive, data-hungry, and powerful neural network architectures.

The Big Three Enablers

  • **Big Data:** The internet explosion provided the necessary fuel for training large models.
  • **GPU Power:** Graphics processing units made parallel computation feasible for deep learning.
  • **Algorithmic Innovation:** Development of architectures like **LSTMs** and the pivotal **Transformer**.

1990s: Machine Learning Dominance

Statistical models (like SVMs) and probabilistic methods began outperforming symbolic systems, emphasizing learning from data.

2012: ImageNet Moment

The use of deep Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) drastically reduced error rates in image recognition, proving the power of deep learning.

2017: The Transformer is Born

The paper "Attention Is All You Need" introduced the Transformer architecture, the basis for all modern Generative AI (GPT, DALL-E, etc.).

Present: Large Language Models (LLMs)

Scaling up the Transformer leads to models with billions of parameters, demonstrating emergent capabilities in reasoning and generation.
